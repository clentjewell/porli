The RealDistrict identity.

One mark, one name set two ways, two greens on paper. This page is the working identity for the concept as it is built into the site: what the pieces are, how they are used, and what not to do with them.

01  The mark

Four blocks, the right column rounded

On paper
On deep olive
Tile, for icons

Construction

Two olive squares on the left, a pale quarter-round top right, an olive quarter-round bottom right with a pale tail tucked under its curve. The gaps are open: the ground shows through, so the mark needs no background of its own.

Colour

On paper, olive and accent. On deep olive, white and a mid green. On a tile, paper and mid green on deep olive. Two greens, never a third colour, never a gradient.

Size and space

It reads at 16px, which is its floor. Keep clear space around it of at least a quarter of its width. Do not rotate it, outline it, or separate the four blocks.

02  The wordmark

Real at text weight, District bold

RealDistrictHeader lock-up
Footer: the mark alone

Setting

One word, no space, set in the interface sans with “Real” at text weight and “District” bold, tracked slightly tight. The two words are one colour. Never all capitals, never split across lines, never a different colour for each half.

With the mark

The mark sits before the name at 0.84 of the name’s size with a gap of 0.3. In the footer the mark stands alone; the name is still set large in the sign-off below it.

In writing

RealDistrict, one word, capital R and capital D. “The RealDistrict team” for the people. Not “Real District”, not “RD”.

Contrast ratios are measured for the pairings the site uses. Clay is an accent for focus and warnings, not a decoration, and colour is never the only signal of a property’s status.

04  Type

One sans, one display serif

Find your next place.

Segoe UI Variable, with Segoe UI, Arial and the system sans behind it. Every control, label and paragraph. Weight and spacing carry the hierarchy; no italics for emphasis, no exaggerated tracking.

Talk to the people who manage it.

EB Garamond, self-hosted, for display headings only. Regular weight, tight tracking, never below 26px. No third-party font services are loaded.

05  Voice and pictures

Plain, warm, and honest about what is real

Words

British English. Short sentences that say what happens: “Ask about this property”, “Request an inspection”, “No homes match these filters.” No luxury language, no superlatives, no promises the team cannot keep.

Labelling

A fictional listing says so on its card and its page. A real property’s photographs are labelled as supplied by the property. A price, room count or area that is not known is stated as to be confirmed, never invented to fill a gap.

Photographs

Natural light, believable scale, straight verticals, restrained grading. No sunsets on every card, no glossy renders, no generic drone shots. A generated image never stands in for a real listed property.
